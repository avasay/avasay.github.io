Blazor Form Validation Part 2: Custom Validation Attributes in Blazor

Alejandrio VasayOctober 6, 2025

In our previous tutorial, we mastered the built-in DataAnnotations for Blazor form validation. But what happens when you need a rule that [Required] or [EmailAddress] can't handle? What if you need to validate that a date is in the future, or that a user has ticked the "Terms & Conditions" checkbox?

This is where Custom Validation Attributes come in. They empower you to extend the validation system with your own business rules, seamlessly integrating into the same, clean workflow you're already using.

In this tutorial, we'll create two practical custom validators and integrate them into a user registration form.

What We'll Build

We will extend a simple registration form with:

1. A [MustBeTrue] attribute to validate acceptance of Terms & Conditions.

2. A [FutureDate] attribute for a booking or event form.

3. Bonus: A simulated [UsernameAvailable] attribute that checks against a static list.

Step 1: Creating the [MustBeTrue] Attribute

This is a very common requirement for checkboxes. The built-in [Range] and [Required] don't work nicely for a bool property that must be true.

Conclusion

You've now unlocked a powerful new tool in your Blazor arsenal. By creating custom validation attributes, you can:

  • Encapsulate Complex Logic: Keep your validation rules neatly packaged and reusable.

  • Maintain Clean Models: Use a clear, declarative syntax on your model properties.

  • Leverage the Full Framework: Your custom attributes work seamlessly with <DataAnnotationsValidator />, <ValidationSummary />, and <ValidationMessage />.
