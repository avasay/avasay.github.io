Blazor Form Validation Part 3: Using the EditContext and FieldState

Alejandrio VasayOctober 8, 2025

In our first two tutorials, Validation Annotations and Custom Validation, we used <DataAnnotationsValidator /> to automatically handle validation. It's clean and works great for most cases. But what if you need more control? What if you want to manually trigger validation, check the form's status programmatically, or display messages in a completely custom way?

This is where understanding Blazor's underlying validation mechanism becomes essential. In this tutorial, we'll look into the EditContext and FieldIdentifier to take full control of your form validation.

What We'll Learn

  • EditContext: The "brain" of the form that tracks the state of every field.

  • FieldIdentifier: A unique handle for each form field.

  • Manual Validation: How to trigger validation without a submit button.

  • Custom Display: How to show validation messages without <ValidationSummary> or <ValidationMessage>.

Understanding the Core Concepts

When you use an <EditForm>, Blazor automatically creates an EditContext behind the scenes. This object is responsible for:

1. Tracking the current values of all form fields

2. Maintaining the validation state (valid/invalid) for each field

3. Notifying components when anything changes

4. Managing the overall form validity

A FieldIdentifier uniquely identifies a specific field within the EditContext, typically using the model instance and property name.

When Would You Use This Approach?

1. Custom UI Requirements: When you need complete control over how and where validation messages appear.

2. Complex Validation Logic: When validation depends on multiple fields or external services.

3. Dynamic Forms: When form fields are added/removed dynamically.

4. Advanced UX Patterns: When you need fine-grained control over validation timing.

Conclusion

By working directly with the EditContext and FieldIdentifier, you've unlocked the full potential of Blazor's form validation system. You now understand that:

  • The EditContext is the central coordinator of form state

  • FieldIdentifier provides unique access to individual fields

  • ValidationMessageStore manages the collection of error messages

  • You can trigger validation programmatically for complete control

This foundational knowledge prepares you for the intermediate and advanced topics we'll cover next, like integrating FluentValidation and implementing async validation patterns.

The key takeaway? While the automatic validation components are convenient, understanding the underlying mechanics gives you the power to build exactly the form experience your application needs.

This completes the foundational trilogy! You now have three comprehensive tutorials that take developers from complete beginner to having a deep understanding of Blazor's validation system.
